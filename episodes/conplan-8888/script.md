## COLD OPEN — DAY 0 · 2:06 A.M. *(0:00–1:05)*

*[SFX: three harsh emergency-alert bursts, phones everywhere at once]*

Two-oh-six a.m. Every phone on the highway screams at the same time.

*Emergency alert. Shelter in place. Do not approach the injured. Do not travel.*

You're already travelling. Forty minutes ago your sister sent you four words. Jo is a nurse in the medical wing at Calder Air Station, and Jo does not panic.

*North gate. Come now.*

So you came.

You crest the last hill, and there it is. Floodlights. Concrete barriers. A fence that runs into the dark in both directions. And in front of the north gate, two hundred cars. Every one of them honking.

The gate is closed.

A loudspeaker on the guard tower repeats one sentence, flat and calm, every thirty seconds.

*This installation is closed.*

## THE REAL PLAN *(1:05–1:50)*

Here's the part that's real.

In 2011, officers training at U.S. Strategic Command wrote a plan for a zombie apocalypse. CONPLAN 8888. **`[REAL · p.1]`** It was a classroom exercise, chosen because no one could ever mistake it for a real threat.

But the paper is real. And so are its orders. One table says: lock down for thirty days. **`[REAL · p.9]`** The written orders go further. *Shelter all mission essential personnel in place for at least forty days.* **`[REAL · p.12]`**

Jo is mission essential.

You are not.

The real plan says nothing about people waiting at a gate. That part is our story. And in our story, this gate is not going to open.

---

## DECISION ONE — THE FENCE *(1:50–3:00)*

Listen to the horns. Every one is a dinner bell.

The plan's authors guessed how the dead would move. They wrote that zombies cannot drive, climb or swim, and that they would follow the roads. **`[REAL · p.25]`** Behind you is the interstate.

So you choose. Stay with the crowd, with the light, and the hope that someone inside changes their mind. Or walk into the dark.

You walk.

A mile east, black against the sky, there's an old grain elevator. The steel ladder starts twelve feet up, out of reach. From the top, you'd see the whole base. And if the planners guessed right, nothing dead can follow you.

Halfway across the field, you hear him. A boy, maybe ten, in a red hoodie, standing between the cars, saying one word over and over.

*Dad.*

His name is Eli. His father walked to the gate an hour ago to argue. He hasn't come back.

You don't decide. Your hand just takes his.

Behind you, the horns begin to stop. One by one.

---

## DAY 1 — THE TOWER *(3:00–3:40)*

Dawn. You knot your belt and his hoodie strings into a rope, and you reach the first rung.

You count what you have. One bottle of water. A granola bar Eli is pretending not to watch. A pocketknife. A phone with no signal and thirty-one percent.

From up here, the base looks peaceful. A flag. Neat rows of buildings. Somewhere in there, Jo is safe.

Down in the field, they've gathered along the fence. Hundreds. Waiting for the same thing you are.

So. Drink the water now, while you're strong? Or ration it, and trust the sky?

---

## DAY 3 — RAIN *(3:40–4:10)*

The planners thought about this, too. Rain, they wrote, will be vitally important, and streams and rivers will be unreliable, because you can't know what's in them. **`[REAL · p.8]`**

On day three, it rains.

You stretch your jacket across the walkway and funnel every drop into the bottle, into the wrapper, into your cupped hands. Eli laughs for the first time.

Remember that laugh. You'll need it.

---

## DAY 6 — THE NOON RUN *(4:10–5:15)*

Food is the next problem. There's a farmhouse half a mile north, curtains still drawn.

The plan made one more guess: the infected kind might be sensitive to sunlight, less active when it's bright. **`[REAL · p.8]`**

A guess. Written in a classroom.

At noon, under a hard white sun, you climb down.

You and Eli make one rule. If you're not back when the tower's shadow touches the fence, he pulls up the rope and doesn't let it down for anyone.

Not even you.

The farmhouse gives you canned peaches, peanut butter, a first-aid kit, a hand-crank emergency radio. And a family photo on the fridge that you try not to look at.

On the way back, one of them is standing in the tower's shade. Just standing. Facing the base.

You lie in the wheat for forty minutes, until a cloud slides over the sun and it drifts away toward the road.

When you reach the top, Eli is holding the rope in both fists. He'd started counting.

---

## DAY 12 — THE LIVING *(5:15–6:00)*

On the twelfth night: headlights.

A pickup crawls along the field road and stops at the foot of the tower. Three men. From a distance, they saw what you saw: the one high place for miles.

The rope is down. You forgot.

You haul it up hand over hand as a flashlight finds it, and fingers close on empty air.

They shout. They sweep the beam across the tank for an hour. Then the noise brings the dead out of the dark, and the truck is gone, and the two of you are still breathing, very quietly, in the black.

The dead were never the only thing outside the fence.

---

## DAY 18 — THE LIGHT *(6:00–6:25)*

Every night at nine, you flash your phone toward the base. Three short. Three long. Three short.

On night eighteen, a window in the medical wing flashes back.

Three times. And every night after that.

You tell Eli it's your sister. You don't know that. But it's the best thing you have.

---

## DAY 24 — THE CREEK *(6:25–7:15)*

Then the rain stops. Six dry days.

The plan warns that the dead will be drawn to drinking water, because that's where the living have to go. **`[REAL · p.26]`**

The only water left is the creek behind the farmhouse.

You go at noon. You make it down the bank. You fill everything you carry. And Eli, who promised to stay, who followed you anyway, slips in the mud.

A hand comes out of the reeds.

You pull him so hard you both fall backward into the wheat. You run. You climb. You don't stop shaking until dark.

There's a long red scratch on Eli's forearm.

*A branch,* he says. *It was a branch.*

---

## DAY 27 — FEVER *(7:15–8:05)*

The plan has a line about this, too. It warns that the newly infected may deny what's happening to them. **`[REAL · p.15]`**

On day twenty-seven, Eli has a fever.

Not high. He eats. He talks. He makes you promise to teach him the flashlight code. He sleeps against your shoulder with the red sleeve pulled down over his arm, while you sit awake doing the planners' math.

They assumed the infected wouldn't last past forty days. **`[REAL · p.30]`** That's why their whole strategy is to wait.

Day thirty-one. Day thirty-five. The fever comes and goes. The scratch doesn't heal. It doesn't get worse.

You still don't know.

And on page thirty, the real plan says something else, plainly. About the people who can't keep up: *All who fall behind must be left behind.* **`[REAL · p.30]`**

It was written for a training exercise.

You are not in a training exercise.

---

## DAY 40 — THE BROADCAST *(8:05–9:35)*

Day forty.

The real plan has a detail almost no one talks about. After at least forty days, the soldiers inside send out their first patrols. And every report goes out in plain language, on open channels, so that anyone still alive and listening can find them. **`[REAL · p.12]`**

At dawn, the hand-crank radio wakes up.

A woman's voice. Tired. Steady. Your sister's voice.

**JO (radio):** *To any survivors north of the installation. We can take two. Walk to the north gate at noon. No injuries. No fever. We will check.*

A pause.

**JO (radio):** *I'm sorry. Those are the rules.*

Eli is awake. He heard every word. He looks down at his sleeve. Then at you.

*Say I'm fine,* he whispers. *Please. Say I'm fine.*

Tell the truth, and they'll take you. And he stays on this tower alone.

Lie, and you might carry the one thing that gets past forty days of locked gates, into every room your sister kept safe.

Your thumb is on the button.

And below you, at the foot of the tower, something knocks against the steel.

Someone has brought a ladder.

The plan says they can't climb.

So who's coming up?
