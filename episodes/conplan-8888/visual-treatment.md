# Visual treatment — "The Military Locked the Gates for 40 Days. You're Outside the Fence."
**Don't Go There! · for review with the script · nothing generated yet**

## 1. The look in one line
Cinematic survival horror (OPTION A, approved): photorealistic cinematic film stills of the American plains, lit only by what's in the scene (floodlights, a phone, a flashlight, the sun). Slow, heavy and quiet, where the camera feels like it's holding its breath.

**How it stays distinct:**
- **From VibeStick:** no 2D animation, no diagrams-as-story, no explainer graphics during the story.
- **From Bunker Annie:** no outlines, no flat colours, no round cartoon heads. Bunker Annie stays the flagship cartoon series; this is the channel's "cinematic file" format.
- **From the niche:** where nearly every breakout is stick figures, this is the only realistic one.

## 2. Palette and light
| Scene type | Palette | Light source |
|---|---|---|
| Night at the base | Sodium-orange floodlights against deep blue-black; fog | Floodlights, headlights, phone screens |
| The tower at night | Near-black with cold moonlight; one warm point (phone or flashlight) | Moon, phone torch, distant base glow |
| Noon runs | Bleached, hard white sun, washed-out gold wheat, short black shadows | Overhead sun |
| Rain (day 3) | Steel grey, wet reflections, a little warmth on faces | Overcast |
| Day 40 dawn | First pink-grey light, radio dial glowing amber | Dawn and radio dial |

- **Accent colour:** Eli's **red hoodie**, the only saturated red in the film, so you can always find him.
- **Grade:** low saturation, teal shadows, warm practical lights, fine film grain, soft vignette.

## 3. Camera and framing
- 16:9, framed like a 2.39:1 film (thin letterbox bars drawn in code) for a cinema feel.
- **"You" is never seen face-on.** Only hands, boots, over-the-shoulder views, a silhouette on the tower, a shadow on the wheat. Clothing is locked for continuity: a dark green canvas jacket, a grey knit beanie, jeans, a wristwatch, a pocketknife. Gender stays ambiguous so every viewer can be "you".
- **Eli** (about 10): a red hoodie with pulled strings, dark curly hair, a thin frame, a too-big backpack. He *is* shown, mostly in profile or partly lit, always readable.
- **Jo:** never clearly seen. A figure in a lit medical-wing window, then only a voice. Her absence is the point.
- **Motion:** every still has a slow push-in (1.00 → 1.06), with occasional slow lateral drifts on wide shots, and hard cuts. Things that move are done in code: fog, rain, flicker, flashlight beams, the radio dial, the phone alert glow, the three-flash signal. No constant shake; one short jolt at the hand in the reeds.

## 4. The dead
- Seen as **silhouettes and shapes**: backlit against floodlights, standing in wheat, swaying at the fence, a single figure in the tower's shade.
- **No gore close-ups,** which keeps it advertiser-friendly. Horror comes from stillness, numbers and wrong posture.
- **The hand in the reeds** is the one close-up: pale, mud-streaked, fast, held for under a second.

## 5. Keeping real facts separate from fiction (on screen)
- **"REAL FILE" inserts** are built in code, not by the image generator, so every word is exact:
  - An off-white typed page in the document's real layout: "UNCLASSIFIED" banner, "CDRUSSTRATCOM CONPLAN 8888-11".
  - The quoted line types on and gets a yellow highlight.
  - A red stamp in the corner: **REAL FILE · p.12 · 2011 TRAINING EXERCISE**.
- **The rule:** only real document text ever appears inside this frame. The story never shows a document.
- **The narrator switches register** at these moments ("Here's the part that's real…", "The real plan says…") and returns to "you" for the story.
- **Story inserts** use a separate small overlay: a stencil day counter top-left (**DAY 24 / 40**) with a thin progress bar toward 40. It is never stamped REAL.
- **End card** spells out real versus fiction (script, last page), and the description lists the page sources.

## 6. Typography
- Day counter and title: a stencil military face (e.g. "Stardos Stencil" or "Black Ops One", OFL) in off-white.
- REAL FILE pages: a typewriter face ("Special Elite" or "Courier Prime", OFL).
- On-screen text stays minimal: day counter, REAL FILE quotes, the alert text on the phone, and the opening title. No captions burned in; an .srt file will be supplied.

## 7. Sound design (your channel style: narration over a low tense drone plus effects)
- A low, slow drone bed that drops out completely before key beats (the hand in the reeds, the knock on the steel).
- Field ambience: wind through wheat, distant crows, insects at night.
- Signature sounds:
  - the triple emergency-alert tone
  - the loudspeaker line
  - two hundred horns fading one by one
  - rain on steel
  - the hand-crank radio winding and static
  - the knock of the ladder on the tank
- **Jo's radio voice:** band-limited and slightly crackly. The narrator stays close and dry.
