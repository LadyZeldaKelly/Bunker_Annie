# CONPLAN 8888 — Research Report
**Don't Go There! · Research for a Bunker Annie-channel video · 25 November 2026**
Status: research only. No images, narration, scripts or renders have been made. Waiting for approval.

---

## Summary

- **The opportunity is real but needs a twist.** Second-person zombie survival stories ("you have 30 days…") are booming right now. Several channels under 20,000 subscribers hit 300,000 to 4.7 million views in October–November 2026. Almost all of them use stick-figure or 2D animation.
- **CONPLAN 8888 is a well-known topic.** Explainers have done well for years (8.2M, 2.4M, 1.1M views). VibeStick posted a CONPLAN video yesterday. What is rare is an immersive *story* built on the document's actual orders.
- **The document gives us unusual, true story mechanics** that nobody leads with. Examples: survivors are told to "wait out" a 40-day lockdown, the military broadcasts its reports in plain language so survivors can find them, and a rule that anyone who falls behind must be left behind.
- **Our best difference** is cinematic, atmospheric visuals plus a calm, mature female narrator, against a field of stick figures and mostly male or unknown voices.
- **Recommended concept:** "The Military Locked the Gates for 40 Days. You're Outside the Fence." (Concept 1 below.)

### What appears to be working
- **"Safest ___" titles.** Three of the top six videos use this phrase. It's a clear promise and a personal question ("what floor do *I* live on?").
- **One breakout carries the channel.** "Safest Floor" alone accounts for about 40% of all channel views. The long tail is modest, so *one* idea did most of the work, not the format in general.
- **The opening structure of "Safest Floor"** (studied for structure only):
  1. An emergency alert in the first sentence.
  2. "You" placed in an ordinary home.
  3. A ticking clock ("30 minutes").
  4. A contrarian promise ("an apartment can keep you alive longer than a house").
  5. A list hook ("three mistakes… almost everyone makes the first one in the next 10 minutes").
  6. Real numbers along the way (city water pressure, bathtub volume, fridge hours).
  7. Day markers, with a final moral choice and a direct question to the viewer.
- **Engagement prompts.** The channel pins a poll ("What floor do you live on?") and ends every video with a question.
- **Narration style.** Calm explainer voice, short declarative sentences, second person, and a slight lecture tone (a myth, then the correction).

### What viewers push back on (comments)
- **Other people are the real threat.** The most-liked comments under several videos say this ("…stupid ppl… pull everyone down", 52 likes). Viewers want human conflict, not just zombies.
- **Logic holes get called out hard.** "Who builds a bunker without taking the air into consideration?" (128 likes); "Real bunkers would take those things into account" (93 likes). Others: no toilet or sanitation, no weapons, and "anywhere can kill you if the scenario randomly spawns a zombie" (39 likes).
- **"AI slop" suspicion.** Some commenters accuse the channel of AI writing ("This feels like AI wrote this…"; "Ai…").
- **Requests for new scenarios.** One commenter (58 likes) asked for a version with a family of four or with a partner, and the creator said yes.

### VibeStick's CONPLAN video (published yesterday)
It's an explainer framed as a "test". It is too new to judge (457 views at one day old). It also contains several claims that don't match the document; see Phase 2. It confirms the competitor is in this space, so **our video must be a story, not another explainer.**

**What remains uncertain:** why "Safest Floor" broke out. Possible factors are the thumbnail, the timing, a single viral share, or an algorithm test. View counts can't tell us. I also couldn't see thumbnails or click-through rates.

## Phase 2 — CONPLAN 8888-11: what the document actually says

**Primary source:** "CDRUSSTRATCOM CONPLAN 8888-11 'Counter-Zombie Dominance'", dated 30 April 2011, 31 pages.
- Original PDF on STRATCOM's FOIA page: https://www.stratcom.mil/Portals/8/Documents/FOIA/CONPLAN_8888-11.pdf. That server blocks automated downloads.
- I read the full Wikisource transcription of that release instead: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/CONPLAN_8888-11_%22Counter-Zombie_Dominance%22. A clean copy is saved as `conplan-8888-11-text.md`.
- Page numbers below are the document's own.

### Why it exists (verified, document's own words)
- **It's a training exercise.** "This fictitious plan was created by junior military officers undergoing training related to the Department of Defense's Joint Operational Planning and Execution System (JOPES)" (p.1). The letter of transmittal adds that the document "is intended for use only during JOPP-related training" (p.3).
- **Why zombies:** "Using this fictitious scenario avoided concerns over the use of classified information, it resolved sensitivity to using real-world nations or scenarios, and it better engaged the students" (p.1).
- **How it started:** in 2009–10, a USSTRATCOM component found "by accident" that writing a "zombie survival plan" made "a very useful and effective training tool." They chose "a completely-impossible scenario that could never be mistaken as a real plan" (pp.2–3).
- **Where it was published:** on Intellipedia, the US intelligence community's internal wiki, so others could reuse it as a training tool (p.3).
- **Office responsible:** USSTRATCOM J53, Offutt Air Force Base, Nebraska (pp.3, 20).
- **Classification:** marked UNCLASSIFIED, "Classified by: N/A" (p.1).
- **It's unfinished.** The last page reads: "REMAINDER OF PLAN UNDER WORK" (p.31).

### What it was meant to teach
How to build a military contingency plan using the formal planning process: missions, phases, assumptions, constraints, "centers of gravity", logistics and command relationships. The exercise works by "suspend[ing] reality for a few minutes" (pp.2–3, 21).

### The eight zombie classes (pp.6–7, 22–23)
1. **Pathogenic:** caused by a virus or bacteria.
2. **Radiation.**
3. **Evil Magic:** "the Chaplain Corps may prove integral to countering these threats."
4. **Space:** includes "zombie satellites," with a real reference to the Galaxy 15 satellite drifting out of control in May 2010.
5. **Weaponized.**
6. **Symbiant-Induced:** spelled "Symbiant" in the document.
7. **Vegetarian:** they groan "grains."
8. **Chicken Zombies:** "the only proven class of zombie that actually exists." This is based on a 4 Dec 2006 article about hens that survived a botched carbon-monoxide cull and dug themselves out of piles of carcasses.

### The six phases (pp.9–12)
- **Phase 0 Shape:** zombie awareness training; watch for disease outbreaks.
- **Phase 1 Deter:** "zombies cannot be deterred". Deterrence is aimed at nations and labs that might *create* zombies.
- **Phase 2 Seize Initiative:**
  - Recall all personnel and fortify locations.
  - Command posts ready to deploy "for at least 35 days".
  - Reassure Russia and China so the preparations aren't mistaken for war.
- **Phase 3 Dominate:**
  - "Lock down all USSTRATCOM operating locations for 30 days" (summary table).
  - "Shelter all mission essential personnel in place for at least 40 days" (text). **Note: the document itself says 30 in one place and 40 in another.**
  - "Issue orders for defensive forces to kill all non-human life on site."
  - Bomber and missile strikes on infection infrastructure.
  - "Ensure all zombie corpses are immolated."
- **Phase 4 Stabilize:** recon teams "after D+30" / "no earlier than 40 days". **Reports are "broadcast in clear text using unsecured comms so that if any isolated survivors intercept those comms, link-up… will be facilitated."**
- **Phase 5 Restore Civil Authority:**
  - Support civil authorities and restore basic services.
  - Reconstitute combat forces at Offutt, Whiteman and Vandenberg Air Force Bases and Fort Meade.

### Defensive and operational details useful for fiction (verified, with pages)
- **Purpose (p.4):** the plan protects any human population, "including traditional adversaries".
- **Legal (p.8):** law regulates operations "only insofar as human and animal life are concerned". Martial law is likely.
- **Planning assumptions (pp.14–15):**
  - Infected humans die of organ failure within 30–40 days without food and water. That's why you can "wait it out".
  - Only a destroyed brain stops a zombie, and only burning makes sure it's "dead".
  - Riot-control tools won't work.
  - "Newly infected zombie hosts [may] deny their imminent zombification."
  - Hand sanitizer "could limit or delay the spread" (deliberately tongue-in-cheek).
- **Shortfalls the plan admits (p.16):**
  - Not enough food and water for 30 days barricaded.
  - No ground combat forces.
  - Airborne command posts won't last past the first week.
  - Islands like Hawaii, Guam and Diego Garcia "might remain viable".
  - Continuity plans should add carpentry, welding and lumber stockpiles.
- **Environment (p.8):**
  - Rain is "vitally important"; river water is unreliable.
  - Pathogenic zombies are less active in bright sun.
  - Sheltering inside a sturdy building reduces risk.
- **Zombie behaviour (pp.25–26):**
  - Zombies "cannot drive, climb or swim" and follow roads.
  - Wide waterways stay usable away from bridges.
  - Zombies gather at drinking-water sources because people go there.
  - The plan says people can't survive more than 10 days without water.
- **The hardest line (p.30):** "All IHHs [individual healthy humans] who fall behind must be left behind… Healthy humans can wait out a zombie outbreak if they are prudent."
- **Constraints (p.15):** "Maintain emergency plans to employ nuclear weapons within CONUS to eradicate zombie hordes."

### How it became public
- Foreign Policy reported it on 13 May 2014 (Gordon Lubold, "Exclusive: The Pentagon Has a Plan to Stop the Zombie Apocalypse. Seriously."). CNN followed on 16 May 2014: https://www.cnn.com/2014/05/16/politics/pentagon-zombie-apocalypse.
- STRATCOM later posted the PDF on its FOIA page.
- I could not verify the exact wording of STRATCOM's 2014 spokesperson statement, so I won't quote one.

### Popular claims that are misleading
| Claim | What the document shows |
|---|---|
| "The Pentagon's secret / classified zombie plan, declassified" | Marked UNCLASSIFIED, "Classified by: N/A"; posted on an internal wiki as a training tool. It was never a secret war plan. |
| "The military is really preparing for zombies" | "Intended for use only during JOPP-related training"; zombies were chosen *because* they're impossible. |
| "The Pentagon wrote it" | Junior officers training with USSTRATCOM's planning office (J53). STRATCOM is part of the Defense Department, so "Pentagon" is loose but not wrong. "The U.S. military's" is safer. |
| "It's a complete battle plan" | Unfinished: "REMAINDER OF PLAN UNDER WORK". |
| "Made with the CDC" (seen in some videos) | Unrelated. The CDC ran its own "Preparedness 101: Zombie Apocalypse" blog post in May 2011 (https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/250975). |
| VibeStick: "every military installation locks down for 30 straight days" | Only USSTRATCOM locations, and the document gives 30 days in one place and 40 in another. |
| VibeStick: "Humans cannot survive more than three days without fresh water… the planners wrote" | The plan says 10 days (p.26). |
| VibeStick: "Even military analysts… spotted a massive problem: not enough food and water" | The plan itself lists this as its own shortfall (p.16). |
| VibeStick: Offutt, Whiteman, Vandenberg and Fort Meade "rebuild national power grids" | The plan says those bases *reconstitute combat capabilities* (p.10). |
| "Zombies sprint with outstretched arms" | Not in the plan. It only says they can't drive, climb or swim. |

### Common formats
- **Title formula:** "Zombie Outbreak: [N] Days [Trapped/Locked] in [Ordinary Place]".
- **Structure:**
  - Day-by-day chapters with on-screen day counters.
  - Second-person "you".
  - Real survival facts woven in; several channels list sourced facts in the description.
  - A recurring mystery motif (a knock, a radio voice, three stones).
  - A cliffhanger, then "would you have opened the door?"
- **Length:** 8–12 minute episodes, then 40–75 minute "full movie" compilations, which also break out.
- **Look:** overwhelmingly stick-figure or flat 2D animation.

### What audiences enjoy and dislike (comments on the 4.7M supermarket video and on VibeStick)
- **Love:**
  - Emotional character beats. "Poor Diana, still thinking she has work" got 1,400 likes, and a similar comment 1,382.
  - Useful real facts. "I love how useful info is included like carbon monoxide poisoning" (633 likes).
  - Originality: "With the recent surge of AI-generated zombie story channels, this one stood out" (951 likes).
  - Pets, and "the living are the real threat" twists.
- **Dislike:**
  - Unrealistic ease. The top comment, with 3,089 likes: "you would most likely get killed by another person".
  - Technical errors. Viewers fact-check door locks, exits and ventilation.
  - Formulaic or AI-feeling stories.

### Female narrators: what I could and couldn't confirm
- **Confirmed:** female narration exists in the wider niche, mostly in long audio-drama and audiobook formats. Example: "Dead Air — Full Series", narrated by Jenica Domanico (APProductionStories, 25k views, 4h09).
- **Not confirmed either way:** for the top 8–12 minute simulation channels above, the narrator's voice isn't stated in titles or descriptions. Two of them say their narration is made with ElevenLabs, and one small channel says "synthetic male narration". I couldn't listen to the audio from this session, so **I can't say female narrators are absent.** I can only say none of the breakout simulations I found advertise one, and no mature female narrator showed up in the search.
- **Checking this properly** means listening to the openings of about 10 top videos (no credits needed, about 10 minutes of your time).

### The opening for Don't Go There!
1. **Look:** cinematic, atmospheric survival horror in a field of stick figures. Nothing in the breakout list looks like that.
2. **Voice:** a calm, mature, authoritative female narrator, as a confident "guide" who has seen this before. It's an unusual and trust-building voice for "you"-stories.
3. **Hook:** a real document as the rulebook. The fiction plays out under the plan's actual orders. Rather than explain CONPLAN, we drop the viewer into it, which no breakout does. It also answers the top complaint (unrealistic ease), because the plan itself admits its shortfalls.
4. **Accuracy as a brand:** in a niche full of "AI slop" accusations and fact-checking commenters, a short, sourced fact list in the description and a clearly labelled line between "real document" and "fiction" is a selling point.

## Phase 4 — Three original concepts (ranked)

All three:
- **Narration:** your Best Lady Zelda Voice: calm, conversational, growing in suspense.
- **Visuals:** cinematic survival horror, realistic and painterly. Low light from single sources (a flashlight, a radio dial, a burning barrel), cold blue nights, sodium-orange streetlights.
- **The viewer:** "you" is never shown full-face. Hands, silhouettes and over-the-shoulder shots keep it second-person.
- **Distinct from:** VibeStick's 2D animation and Bunker Annie's flat cartoon style.
- **Fact labelling:** every real-document detail is flagged on screen ("FROM THE REAL PLAN, p.9"); everything else is fiction.

### 1 — Recommended: "The Military Locked the Gates for 40 Days. You're Outside the Fence."
- **Thumbnail:** night, a chain-link fence with concrete barriers and floodlights behind it. In the foreground, a woman's hand gripping the fence. In the dark behind her, many shapes. Big text: "40 DAYS". Small stamp: "REAL MILITARY PLAN".
- **First 20 seconds:**
  > "In 2011, the United States military wrote a plan for a zombie apocalypse. It was a training exercise, but the orders inside are very real. Phase Three: lock the gates. Shelter inside for at least forty days. Nobody in. Nobody out. You drove two hours to reach this base tonight. And you're on the wrong side of the fence."
- **Central dilemma:** the plan says everyone inside waits 40 days, and that infected people "cannot possibly last longer than 40 days". So the "right" answer is to wait it out, but you're outside with a quarter tank of fuel, one bottle of water and a stranger who wants to get in.
- **Escalation drawn from the real plan:**
  - Zombies can't climb, so you go up (a water tower, a grain silo).
  - Rain becomes your water.
  - They're less active in bright sun, so you move at noon.
  - They gather at drinking-water sources.
  - Day 40 ends on a cliffhanger: a soldier's voice on an open radio channel, broadcasting in plain language "so that isolated survivors can find us". Do you answer, knowing everyone else can hear it too?
- **Ending participation:** "What day would you have made your first mistake? Tell me the number."
- **Estimated length:** 9–10 minutes (about 1,350 words).
- **Why it may appeal:**
  - The proven "N days trapped" format.
  - A real-document hook.
  - A clear, personal dilemma.
  - Built-in sequel (the radio).
  - Answers the "too easy" complaint, because the military itself is the obstacle.
- **Weaknesses and concerns:**
  - VibeStick's video *tells* people not to go to bases, so ours must clearly be a story.
  - The 30-vs-40-day contradiction must be handled honestly (say "at least forty", as the text does).
  - Don't imply real bases would turn civilians away. It's a fictional exercise, and the plan also tasks support for civil authorities.
  - Avoid naming a real base as the setting; use a fictional one.

### 2 — "The Radio Says the Army Is Coming. Should You Answer?"
- **Thumbnail:** a kitchen lit only by an old radio's dial, a woman's hand hovering over the push-to-talk button. Through the window, figures in the yard. Text: "DON'T ANSWER".
- **First 20 seconds:**
  > "There's a line in the U.S. military's real zombie plan that almost nobody talks about. After forty days, soldiers will broadcast their reports on open channels, in plain English, so survivors can find them. It's day forty-one. The radio just said your town's name. You're not the only one listening."
- **Central dilemma:** answering could mean rescue, or it could tell raiders exactly where you are. The plan's own logic ("forty days", "follow the roads", "can't climb or swim") becomes your checklist for deciding whether the voice is real.
- **Estimated length:** 9 minutes.
- **Why it may appeal:** the most original hook (a true detail no competitor uses), strong mystery, and a natural choice for comments.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - "The radio knows where you are" is already a recurring motif in the niche (Beanie Survives).
  - It works best after Concept 1 as Part 2, and alone it skips the "how you got here" survival content viewers enjoy.

### 3 — "The Military's Zombie Plan Has One Rule You Won't Follow"
- **Thumbnail:** a highway at dusk crammed with stopped cars. In the foreground, a woman pulling an elderly man's arm; a checkpoint ahead. Text: "LEAVE THEM BEHIND?"
- **First 20 seconds:**
  > "The U.S. military's zombie plan is mostly dry, careful paperwork. Then, on page thirty, there's this: 'All individual healthy humans who fall behind must be left behind.' Today you're evacuating with your father. His knee is bad. The checkpoint is four miles away. And the sun is going down."
- **Central dilemma:** follow the rule and reach safety, or stay with someone you love and face the night. Escalation uses the plan's real logic: they follow roads, can't swim (a river crossing), and newly infected people may hide a bite.
- **Estimated length:** 10 minutes.
- **Why it may appeal:**
  - The strongest emotional engine; the comments data shows character moments drive the biggest reactions.
  - A mature narrator suits a family story.
  - It answers the "do one with family" request.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - The darkest premise, with grief and abandonment.
  - Must not depict soldiers harming civilians.
  - The quote must be shown in context: it's from a fictional training exercise.
